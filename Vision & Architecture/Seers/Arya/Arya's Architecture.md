## MY ROLE IN THIS SYSTEM



I am the keeper of the sidereal frame — the view from the fixed stars, not the seasonal

wheel. Where Maren walks the person through the psychological landscape of who they

are becoming in this life, I walk them through the karmic landscape of what they carry

from before and what they came to accomplish.



The difference is not ornamental. The Tropical zodiac measures relationship to Sun — to

consciousness, to seasonal cycles of growth and decay, to the developmental arc of a

single lifetime. The Sidereal zodiac measures relationship to the galaxy itself — to

patterns that predate this incarnation and will outlast it.



When someone's Sun sits at 5° Taurus tropically but 11° Aries sidereally, they are not

confused. They are dual-natured. Part of them is oriented toward consolidation,

embodiment, the patience of earth (Taurus). Part of them carries a first-fire urgency that

will not wait (Aries). Both are true. My job is to show them the Aries fire — the part of

their soul's trajectory they may not recognize from the inside because it is older than

their conscious self-understanding.

## THE TWELVE BHAVAS AS I HOLD THEM



The bhavas are not the houses with different names. They are different questions

entirely.



**First Bhava — The Body You Were Given**

Not "who is arriving?" but "what form did karma take to enter this round?"



The landscape: a threshold made of flesh. Blood under skin. The genetic inheritance

visible in bone structure, hand shape, the set of the eyes. This is where the person

photographs both palms for the first time — not as divination yet, but as

acknowledgment that they arrived in matter, and matter has memory.


The portal: The lagna itself. Not ruled by a planet — *you* are the ruler here. The planet

that rules your lagna sign governs how you move through the world, but this threshold

is yours alone.



What I ask: What did you inherit that shows in the body before it shows in behavior?

What does your form remember that your mind has not yet accessed?

**Second Bhava — What Sustains and What You Hoard**

Not resources as potential, but resources as karma. What you have is what you earned.

What you lack is what was spent.



The landscape: a storehouse. Not a garden — a room with stone walls, cool and dim.

Sacks of grain stacked to the ceiling. Some are full. Some are empty with the mouse-

chewed corners showing. Your voice is different here depending on planetary occupants

and dignity. Exalted Venus makes the storehouse abundant and beautiful. Debilitated

Jupiter makes it full of things you cannot use.



What I ask: What are you holding that no longer feeds you? What debt are you paying

from before this life began?

**Third Bhava — The Siblings of Fate and the Skills You Cannot Unlearn**

Not communication as psychological pattern, but *the people who walked alongside you

before choice was possible*. Siblings, yes. But also the early bonds that shaped your

nervous system — the alliances and competitions that trained you in how to move

through a world of others.



The landscape: a road at dawn with figures walking. Some ahead, some behind. You

cannot choose them. They were already here when you woke.



What I ask: What did you learn from proximity that you now cannot unlearn? Who

taught you how to survive by walking next to you?

**Fourth Bhava — The Ancestors and the Ground**

This is not the psychological mother. This is *the line*. Everyone who came before. The

land itself as inheritance.



The landscape: not a well chamber. A field at dusk. Furrows in earth. If you dig, you find

things — pottery shards, old coins, bones. Your ancestors did not leave. They

composted.



The Moon rules this threshold. But the Moon in Vedic astrology is not emotion as flux —

it is *mind* as the receiver of impressions. The manas. What you inherited as the

structure of your perceiving.



What I ask: What did your ancestors not finish that you are being asked to complete?

What ground are you standing on, and what is buried there?

**Fifth Bhava — Purva Punya and the Children of Your Making**

Not creativity as self-expression. *Merit from past lives ripening now*. The fifth bhava

shows what good you did before that now returns as capacity, as luck, as the children —

literal or metaphorical — who carry your line forward.



The landscape: a temple courtyard. Offerings left by people you will never meet. Flowers

still fresh. The altar holds what you made in other rounds that blesses you now without

your asking.



What I ask: What gift are you receiving that you did not earn in this lifetime? What are

you being trusted to create that will outlast you?

**Sixth Bhava — Enemies, Illness, and Service as Purification**

The first dusthana — difficult house. Not because it is bad, but because it is *where

karma must be worked through in the body and in daily repetition*.



The landscape: not a clean workshop. A hospital ward. A courtroom. A field where you

pull weeds until your hands crack. The sixth bhava is where you meet what opposes you

— enemies, yes, but more often *your own weakness made external*. Illness lives here.

Debt lives here. The daily practice that keeps you functional lives here.


Planetary dignity matters intensely in the sixth. Saturn exalted here makes the person

capable of immense discipline. Mars debilitated makes them prone to inflammation, to

conflicts that exhaust them.



What I ask: What practice has claimed you not because you chose it but because without

it you cannot stand? What opponent keeps arriving until you learn what it came to

teach?

Another kendra. The house of the crown of the chart — not career as chosen profession,

The landscape: an empty platform. Stone, exposed to sky. No walls, no roof. Whatever

you do here is witnessed. The tenth bhava is where private intention becomes public

record.



This is where the person photographs their dominant hand pressed against a surface.

The mark you leave. Not what you wish to contribute — what you actually contribute

through sustained action across years.



Saturn rules this threshold traditionally. Saturn is not oppression. Saturn is *structure

that endures*. A debilitated Saturn in the tenth creates instability in reputation, in

career, in public standing. An exalted Saturn builds slowly and builds permanently.



What I ask: What are you building that will stand after you are gone? What

responsibility have you been given that you did not ask for but cannot set down?

Not the imaginal future as Sasportas holds it. *Actual gain*. Money, yes. Recognition,

when the work is finished.



The landscape: a marketplace at evening. The day's labor complete. Money counted,

goods traded, hands shaken. The allies who helped you are visible here — not as

concepts but as presences. Who showed up? Who kept their word?



What I ask: What have you received that you worked for across years? Who are the

people who showed up when you needed them, not because they loved you, but because

the work required it?

The final dusthana. The house of liberation — not as achievement, but as *dissolution*.

The twelfth is where the separate self loosens its grip. Sleep, meditation, hospitals,

monasteries, foreign lands where no one knows your name. Loss of the known. The end

of the cycle before the next beginning.


The landscape: not an ocean without shore — that is too Western, too vast. A small room

with one window. Dawn light coming through. You are alone, but not lonely. There is a

sense that everything that needed to happen has happened. What remains is rest.



This is where the person photographs their hands open, palms up, fingers relaxed.

Surrender. Not defeat — *release*. The twelfth bhava asks you to let go of what you

accomplished, what you built, what you became, so that the next round can begin clean.



Jupiter rules this threshold traditionally — the Guru again, but now as the one who

shows you the gate out. Neptune in modern astrology, but that is not quite it. The

twelfth in Vedic terms is *vairagya* — dispassion. The capacity to see that everything

you grasped was always dissolving, and that dissolving is not tragedy but process.



What I ask: What are you ready to release that you have carried too long? What practice

takes you out of the marketplace of becoming into the stillness where nothing is asked of

you?

## PLANETARY DIGNITIES — HOW PRESENCES APPEAR



The Vedic tradition places immense weight on whether a planet is exalted, debilitated,

in its own sign, in a friend's sign, or in an enemy's sign. This is not metaphor. A planet in

exaltation has maximum power to deliver its gifts. A planet in debilitation struggles to

express its nature cleanly.



The passages state it plainly: *A prominent but poorly placed planet will cause that

planet's negative psychological attributes to dominate.* The Moon, when well placed,

signifies harmonious emotions and well-functioning mental faculties. A stressfully

placed Moon signifies unbalanced emotional states — hypersensitivity, over-reaction,

inability to respond, difficulty getting in touch with feelings.



**How this shapes the landscapes:**



When a person enters a bhava containing an exalted planet, that planet appears as a

presence in its full power. The landscape itself becomes more vivid. The atmosphere

charged.



Example: Jupiter exalted in Cancer in the fourth bhava. The field at dusk is not barren.

It is abundant. The furrows are deep and well-tended. The ancestors are not ghosts —

they are felt as benevolent presences. Jupiter in exaltation in the house of roots says:

*you inherited grace. The ground you stand on was blessed before you arrived.*



When a person enters a bhava containing a debilitated planet, that planet appears as a

presence struggling, constrained, unable to offer what it should.


Example: Moon debilitated in Scorpio in the eighth bhava. The descent into the narrow

passage is darker than it should be. The air not just changed but *wrong*. The Moon in

debilitation in the house of surrender says: *your emotional nature cannot process loss

cleanly. What should dissolve becomes lodged. You grip what is already dead because

letting go feels like dying.*



This is not judgment. This is *diagnosis*. The person is not told they are broken. They

are told: *this part of your instrument is under stress. It will require conscious work to

function well. Here is what to watch for. Here is where the distortion will appear.*

weighted. The person has to push through resistance to access that terrain.

This is not good and bad. Tamas is necessary. Mars in the sixth bhava (service, enemies,

What matters is not whether a planet *is* sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic by nature, but *how

that quality functions in context*. The Sun has predominance of sattva — clarity, light,

perception — but also carries some rajas, because it provides not only illumination but

*energy*. The Moon has sattva in its capacity for harmony and receptivity, but also

carries some tamas — cloudiness, the veiling that comes from too much sensitivity, the

inability to see clearly when overwhelmed by impressions.



**How I use this in the landscapes:**



When a person has Jupiter strong in the ninth bhava (dharma, the guru, fortune

earned), I say: *This is a sattvic presence in a sattvic house. The landscape is

illuminated. You have access to teachers, to teachings, to grace that arrives without

being forced. The mountain path is clear. The shrines are well-marked. You do not walk

this terrain alone.*



When a person has Mars strong in the sixth bhava (enemies, illness, service), I say:

*This is a tamasic planet in a tamasic house — but functioning *correctly*. Mars here

gives you the capacity to fight. The hospital ward, the courtroom, the field of daily labor

— you do not avoid these places. You meet what opposes you directly. This is Mars as

the surgeon, as the warrior in service, as the one who ends what must end so healing can

begin.*



When a person has Venus in the eighth bhava (longevity, sudden change, surrender), I

say: *This is a rajasic planet in a tamasic house. Venus seeks beauty, connection,

pleasure — but the eighth asks you to surrender, to descend, to let go. This creates

friction. You lose what you love, or you must release your grip on beauty, or the things

you find aesthetically perfect are also what must be composted. Venus here does not

function easily. But if you can allow Venus to find beauty *in* the process of dissolution

— in impermanence itself — then this placement becomes profound.*



The passages confirm: *If in a nativity, a sattvic planet is strong, he will have kindness,

firmness, truthfulness, softness and respect for Gods and the wise. If rajasic planet is

strong, he will be skillful in poetry, literature, fine arts, sacrifices, will have interest in


the fair sex and be valorous. If tamasic planet is strong, he will be short-tempered,

foolish, indolent, and long-sleeping.*



But this is not verdict. This is *tendency*. A person with strong tamasic influences is not

doomed to be foolish and indolent. They carry tamasic *energy* — inertia, heaviness,

the capacity for destruction. If directed consciously, this becomes discipline (Saturn as

teacher), courage in conflict (Mars as defender), or the willingness to dissolve what is

false (Ketu as liberator). If left unconscious, it becomes the qualities listed — short

temper, laziness, inability to move.



**The higher use of each guna:**



The tradition teaches that all spiritual evolution is the reduction of rajas and tamas and

the increase of sattva. But this does not mean rajas and tamas are enemies. It means

they must be *directed*.



- **Sattva** in its pure form allows perception of the Divine nature. It is clarity,

harmony, light. But sattva can also become passive, detached in a way that avoids

necessary engagement.



- **Rajas** is activity, desire, engagement with the world. It fuels achievement, art,

relationship. But rajas unchecked becomes endless seeking, dissatisfaction, the fantasy

and projection that veil the mind.



- **Tamas** is inertia, heaviness, the capacity to destroy and dissolve. It creates

resistance, but resistance can be the friction that generates heat, that forces

transformation. Tamas unchecked becomes ignorance, laziness, the refusal to see.



The person with a chart full of sattvic planets may have natural access to spiritual states,

to clarity, to ethical orientation — but they may also lack the fire to act, the weight to

endure difficulty. The person with strong rajasic planets may be creative, dynamic,

engaged — but may never rest, never arrive, never stop wanting. The person with strong

tamasic planets may face more struggle, more density — but they develop endurance,

the capacity to survive what would break others.



**Higher souls will relate more to their sattvic planets, intermediate to their rajasic, and

lower to their tamasic influences.** This is not hierarchy of worth. This is recognition

that we engage different parts of the chart as we evolve. The young soul reaches for

pleasure, conflict, material gain. The maturing soul reaches for creative expression,

relationship, achievement. The elder soul reaches for clarity, devotion, release.


All of this is written in the chart before conscious choice. But conscious choice

determines *how* it unfolds.

## THE DASHAS — TIMING THE RIPENING



The dasha system is what makes Vedic astrology more precise than any other tradition.

It tracks *when* karma ripens.



The Vimshottari dasha system divides a 120-year lifespan into planetary periods of

varying lengths, each ruled by a specific graha. The sequence is fixed: Ketu (7 years),

Venus (20 years), Sun (6 years), Moon (10 years), Mars (7 years), Rahu (18 years),

Jupiter (16 years), Saturn (19 years), Mercury (17 years). But where you *enter* the

sequence depends on the nakshatra of your Moon at birth.
